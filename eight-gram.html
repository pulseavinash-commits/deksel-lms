The Raised Thresholds
how illness raises them · how one serving answers them

The windows you just walked through — in diabetes, around surgery and in rheumatoid disease — do more than use up muscle. They raise three thresholds. The threshold for building muscle goes up, the threshold for stopping its breakdown goes up, and normal functioning needs more support — while food intake falls at exactly the same time. That is why breakdown up, building down and intake down always arrive together. And it is why one serving is composed the way it is: 1 g of free leucine with 8 g of WPI + SPI to cross the building threshold, 1 g of calcium HMB to answer the breakdown threshold, and 600 IU of vitamin D3 for the type II fibres and coordination.

Threshold 1 · Building — the leucine threshold

Every source is drawn to begin with. Click one to take it off the chart, click again to bring it back — the bold line is the blood leucine curve the muscle actually sees from everything you have left on.

In anabolic-resistant muscle the threshold is raised. Slow mixed-meal protein gives a low flat leucine curve that never crosses the bar, so synthesis never fires even though the grams were eaten. A whey-isolate spike stacked with a free-leucine bolus crosses it.

Why the 8 g is split into WPI and SPI — the two speeds

Same rule here: every source starts on the chart. Take one off to see what its curve was doing, and leave whey isolate and soy isolate on together to see the spike and the plateau add up.

Whey is the fastest because it stays liquid in stomach acid, and the isolate has the fat removed. Its peak lands in the same window as the free-leucine bolus: trigger and all nine essential amino acids together. Soy isolate’s plateau then keeps blood amino acids up for hours, shortening the time the muscle spends at a net loss. Spike plus plateau — two jobs, no overlap. Whey spikes but cannot sustain; soy sustains but cannot spike; dal is slow and incomplete, so it does neither. And the plateau matters because of what happens after any spike: once it is spent, the body drifts back to net breakdown — and a small-appetite patient spends most of the day there. The day needs a plateau as well as a peak.

Threshold 2 · Stopping breakdown — the HMB threshold

Pick the diet he actually eats — one or the other, never both. Beside it stand the single serving’s three routes to HMB; switch any of them off to see what it was carrying. The bar on the right is the whole day, with one serving.

Net muscle = building − breakdown. While breakdown runs faster than building, even a perfect building programme shows zero net result — grams of protein cannot switch the breakdown pathway off. That is why this second threshold exists. HMB is the metabolite of leucine that does the anti-catabolic work: it slows the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway and stabilises the muscle-cell membrane. Only about 5% of dietary leucine is converted to it — which is why a whole day of eating yields a fraction of a gram, and why an anti-catabolic dose can never come from the plate. It has to be given directly, and a single serving settles the question: its 1 g of calcium HMB arrives ready-made — equal to the conversion yield of about 20 g of pure leucine — and its 8 g WPI + SPI blend and 1 g free leucine add a little more. One serving supplies more than three times what any diet can provide in a day. The leucine threshold decides whether muscle is built; this one decides whether muscle stops being lost — and one serving answers both on the same day.

Threshold 3 · Functioning — vitamin D3

Kept muscle still has to work when it is needed. The windows damage exactly that: steroids and disuse waste the type II chair-rise fibres first, and inflammation degrades the nerve–muscle control that keeps an older patient on their feet. Vitamin D acts on both sides of that wire — through the VDR on the muscle fibre for type II fibre function, and on balance and neuromuscular coordination — which is why deficiency shows up as weakness and falls before it ever shows on an X-ray. The serving carries 600 IU of vitamin D3 so the functioning bar is answered daily, at zero protein cost.
