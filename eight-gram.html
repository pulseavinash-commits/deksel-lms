The Leucine Threshold
and the two protein speeds

Two pieces of physiology decide whether eaten protein ever becomes muscle. First, how high the leucine bar sits — muscle building fires only when blood leucine crosses a threshold, and illness raises that threshold. Second, how fast the amino acids arrive — a curve that rises slowly never crosses a raised bar, however many grams were on the plate.

The leucine threshold — same grams, different biology

In anabolic-resistant muscle the threshold is raised. Slow mixed-meal protein gives a low flat leucine curve that never crosses the bar, so synthesis never fires even though the grams were eaten. A whey-isolate spike stacked with a free-leucine bolus crosses it.

↑

The raised bar

Young muscle fires on a normal meal (~1.5–2 g leucine). Resistant muscle needs a bigger, faster bolus to fire mTOR at all. “Eat more protein” fails because of the mechanism, not because the patient did not comply.

✂

The unsealed leak

Net muscle = synthesis − breakdown. While breakdown runs faster than building, a perfect anabolic program still shows zero net result. Grams cannot switch the demolition pathway off.

5%

The HMB arithmetic

The ~3 g clinical HMB dose would need about 60 g of pure leucine — kilograms of protein food every day. No powder, however premium, can deliver it.

◑

The unprotected hours

Once a spike is spent, the body drifts back to net breakdown — and a small-appetite patient spends most of the day there. The day needs a plateau as well as a peak.

The protein speed spectrum — why two proteins are needed

Whey is the fastest because it stays liquid in stomach acid, and the isolate has the fat removed. Its peak lands in the same window as the free-leucine bolus: trigger and all nine essential amino acids together. Soy isolate’s plateau then keeps blood amino acids up for hours, shortening the time the muscle spends at a net loss. Spike plus plateau — two jobs, no overlap. Casein can sustain but cannot spike (and it clots); whey spikes but cannot sustain; dal is slow and incomplete.

The two sentences together

“The bar is higher in this patient, so the curve has to be steeper. A whey-isolate spike stacked with free leucine is what crosses the raised threshold and fires building. A soy-isolate plateau behind it keeps amino acids available through the hours when the body would otherwise drift back into net breakdown. One protein cannot do both jobs — the spike and the plateau are different pharmacology, not a bigger and a smaller dose of the same thing.”
